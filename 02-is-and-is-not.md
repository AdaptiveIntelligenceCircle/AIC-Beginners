# 02 — What AIC is and is not

This file is intentionally blunt. Its purpose is to reduce projection and prevent well-intentioned contributors from over-claiming.

---

## AIC is

| Statement | Clarification |
|-----------|---------------|
| An **open design and documentation effort** | Public repositories, principles, and phase plans are the primary artifacts. |
| **Pre-Covenant** | Covenant criteria and go-live checklists exist as documents; they have not been declared satisfied. |
| Oriented toward **ethical coordination** and **meaning infrastructure** ideas | Focus is on constraints, recovery, human gates, and under-claim narrative—not on maximizing throughput or TVL. |
| **Fail-closed by design intent** | Uncertainty and missing evidence should produce restriction, not expansion. |
| **Independent and non-profit-oriented in stated intent** | No token rule-power; fundraising or entity form (if any) does not buy governance authority. |
| Subject to ordinary law | Entity ≠ immunity. |

---

## AIC is not

| Claim to reject | Why |
|-----------------|-----|
| A launched mainnet or production protocol | No such declaration exists under the Covenant criteria. |
| A cryptocurrency or token-governed DAO | Explicit design rule: no token rule-power. |
| A religion, cult, or personality vehicle | Founder and contributors are not sources of immunity or revelation. |
| A guarantee of safety, fairness, or global adoption | Under-claim posture forbids such guarantees. |
| A bypass for national law (including AI regulation) | See safety-and-law notes; local law remains binding. |
| An immune legal entity | Any associated organization remains accountable. |
| A finished “World Meaning Infrastructure” | WMI is a design direction, not a deployed global system. |
| A replacement for democratic or legal process | Coordination protocol ideas do not supersede law. |

---

## Common misreadings to avoid

1. **“It’s like Ethereum but ethical.”**  
   Surface analogies are limited. AIC materials explicitly contrast with token-centric governance and market capture of rules.

2. **“Once the code is good enough, we declare Covenant.”**  
   Covenant is multi-criteria (technical, governance, disclosure, recovery, human gates). Code alone is insufficient.

3. **“The founder / entity can decide exceptions.”**  
   Written constraints and fail-closed posture are meant to limit that. Entity ≠ immunity.

4. **“We can soft-launch and fix ethics later.”**  
   Design intent is the opposite: constraints first, expansion later only under clear criteria.

---

## How to use this file

- When writing docs, issues, or public statements, check against the “is not” column.
- When someone asks “is AIC going to …?”, prefer linking here or to the relevant phase/Covenant document over improvising.
- If you believe a public claim has drifted into over-claim territory, raise it as a documentation or governance issue with references.
