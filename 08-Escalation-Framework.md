# AIC Contributor Escalation Framework

**Version:** 1.0

**Status:** Active

**Scope:** All contributors, maintainers, researchers, and community members.

---

# Purpose

Adaptive Intelligence Circle (AIC) is an institution-building ecosystem.

Escalation exists to support learning, coordination, and recovery.

Escalation is not a shortcut around investigation.

Contributors should attempt to understand the problem before requesting intervention.

The objective is:

```text
Problem
↓
Investigation
↓
Knowledge Sharing
↓
Escalation (if required)
↓
Resolution
↓
Institutional Memory
```

---

# Escalation Levels

AIC defines three escalation levels.

## Level 1 — Self Investigation

### Owner

Contributor

### Objective

Build understanding before involving others.

### Expected Actions

Before escalating:

- Reproduce the issue.
- Gather logs and outputs.
- Review documentation.
- Search existing Issues.
- Review previous Pull Requests.
- Create at least one hypothesis.

### Questions

The contributor should attempt to answer:

- What happened?
- What was expected?
- What changed?
- What do I believe is causing the issue?

### Escalation Criteria

Escalate only if:

- Investigation has been performed.
- Findings are documented.
- No clear resolution has been identified.

---

## Level 2 — Community Escalation

### Owner

Maintainers and Contributors

### Objective

Leverage collective intelligence.

### Process

The contributor opens:

- Issue
- Discussion
- Technical Question

including:

- Context
- Investigation steps
- Logs
- Current hypothesis
- Reproduction steps

### Community Responsibilities

Participants should:

- Review findings.
- Suggest experiments.
- Share previous experiences.
- Propose alternative explanations.

Avoid:

- Guessing without evidence.
- Immediate reassignment to Founder.
- Unverified assumptions.

### Escalation Criteria

Escalate only if:

- Multiple contributors cannot resolve the issue.
- Architectural ambiguity exists.
- Governance concerns emerge.
- Safety or security implications appear.

---

## Level 3 — Governance / Foundation Escalation

### Owner

Founder, Chief Architect, Governance Team, or designated stewards.

### Objective

Resolve ecosystem-level issues.

### Examples

Level 3 includes:

- Architectural conflicts.
- Governance disputes.
- Repository ownership questions.
- Contributor conduct concerns.
- Security incidents.
- Foundation policy decisions.
- Strategic roadmap conflicts.

### Required Inputs

The escalation request must contain:

- Prior investigation.
- Community discussion reference.
- Alternative solutions considered.
- Impact assessment.
- Recommendation (if any).

### Expected Outcome

Possible outcomes:

- Technical direction.
- Governance ruling.
- Documentation update.
- Policy clarification.
- Working group formation.
- Deferred decision.

# Escalation Philosophy

Escalation is not a failure.

However:

Escalation without investigation slows institutional learning.

AIC encourages contributors to:

```text
Investigate First

Document Second

Escalate Third
```

rather than:

```text
Escalate First

Investigate Later
```

---

# Institutional Learning Rule

Every Level 2 or Level 3 resolution should improve at least one of the following:

- Documentation
- Onboarding Materials
- Governance Notes
- Architecture Records
- Knowledge Base
- Contributor Guidelines

A solved problem should become easier for future contributors.

If knowledge remains only with individuals, the institution does not learn.

---

# Long-Term Objective

The objective of AIC is to create contributors capable of:

- Independent investigation.
- Critical thinking.
- Technical reasoning.
- Knowledge sharing.
- Mentoring others.

The healthiest institution is not the one where the Founder solves every problem.

The healthiest institution is the one where contributors can increasingly solve problems together.
