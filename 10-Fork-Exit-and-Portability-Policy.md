# Fork, Exit and Portability Policy

## Purpose

Adaptive Intelligence Circle (AIC) recognizes that freedom, experimentation, and autonomy are essential elements of a healthy open ecosystem.

This policy defines the rights and responsibilities related to:

- Forking projects.
- Exiting the ecosystem.
- Knowledge portability.
- Contributor autonomy.

---

## Principle of Freedom

AIC supports software freedom and open collaboration.

Contributors retain the freedom to:

- Leave the ecosystem.
- Create alternative implementations.
- Fork repositories where licensing permits.
- Develop independent projects.

Participation in AIC is voluntary.

No contributor is required to remain within the ecosystem.

---

## Principle of Respectful Exit

Contributors choosing to leave AIC are encouraged to:

- Provide reasonable notice when feasible.
- Document ongoing work.
- Transfer relevant information where appropriate.
- Avoid unnecessary disruption.

Leaving AIC is not considered disloyalty.

Healthy institutions allow healthy exits.

---

## Forking Rights

Where licensing permits, contributors may fork repositories and develop alternative implementations.

Forks are recognized as a legitimate part of open ecosystems.

Forking should not automatically be interpreted as hostility.

Forking can serve as:

- Experimentation.
- Research.
- Governance divergence.
- Technical exploration.

---

## Responsible Forking

Fork creators are encouraged to:

- Respect license terms.
- Clearly communicate differences.
- Avoid misleading branding.
- Maintain transparency regarding objectives.

Users should be able to distinguish between:

```text
Official AIC Projects
```

and

```text
Independent Forks
```

without confusion.

---

## Foundation Assets

The following may have separate governance requirements:

- Foundation trademarks.
- Official visual identities.
- Governance documents.
- Foundation-owned infrastructure.

Open-source rights do not automatically convey stewardship rights over institutional assets.

---

## Knowledge Portability

AIC believes knowledge should remain portable.

Whenever practical:

- Documentation should remain accessible.
- Decisions should be recorded.
- Processes should be transparent.
- Institutional memory should not depend on a single individual.

No critical system should rely exclusively on one contributor.

---

## Founder and Contributor Independence

The Founder does not own the ecosystem's contributors.

Contributors do not own the Foundation.

Both participate in a shared mission while maintaining independent agency.

---

## Fork as Governance Signal

A fork should be viewed as information.

It may indicate:

- Technical disagreement.
- Governance disagreement.
- Different priorities.
- Alternative experimentation.

Forks provide feedback about the health of the ecosystem.

---

## Long-Term Objective

AIC seeks to create an ecosystem where:

```text
Freedom
+
Responsibility
+
Transparency
+
Institutional Continuity
```

can coexist.

The strength of an institution is not measured by its ability to prevent exits.

The strength of an institution is measured by whether people freely choose to remain, collaborate, and continue building together.
