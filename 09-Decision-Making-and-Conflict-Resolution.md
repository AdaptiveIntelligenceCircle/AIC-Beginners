# Decision-Making and Conflict Resolution

## Purpose

Adaptive Intelligence Circle (AIC) recognizes that disagreement is a natural consequence of innovation, research, and collaborative development.

Conflict itself is not considered a failure.

Failure occurs when disagreements cannot be resolved constructively or when institutional learning stops.

This policy establishes principles for decision-making and conflict resolution within the AIC ecosystem.

---

## Governance Principles

Decision-making within AIC shall be guided by the following principles:

- Transparency whenever possible.
- Respect for evidence and documentation.
- Long-term institutional benefit over short-term convenience.
- Human dignity and constructive communication.
- Preference for consensus before authority.
- Adaptation through learning and iteration.

---

## Decision-Making Levels

### Level 1 — Contributor Decisions

Contributors are encouraged to make routine decisions related to:

- Code implementation.
- Documentation improvements.
- Issue management.
- Repository maintenance activities.

Contributors should document significant decisions whenever possible.

---

### Level 2 — Maintainer Decisions

Maintainers may decide on:

- Technical direction within repositories.
- Acceptance or rejection of contributions.
- Architectural consistency.
- Standards and implementation choices.

Maintainers should provide reasoning for significant decisions.

---

### Level 3 — Foundation Decisions

The Founder, Chief Architect, Governance Stewards, or designated governance bodies may decide on:

- Ecosystem-wide architecture.
- Governance policy.
- Contributor conduct matters.
- Strategic priorities.
- Funding allocation.
- Institutional partnerships.
- Foundation structure.

These decisions should be documented whenever appropriate.

---

## Consensus-Seeking Rule

Before escalation, contributors should attempt good-faith discussion.

The preferred sequence is:

```text
Discussion
↓
Clarification
↓
Consensus
↓
Decision
↓
Implementation
```

rather than:

```text
Authority
↓
Compliance
```

AIC values understanding over coercion.

---

## Conflict Resolution Process

### Stage 1 — Direct Communication

Parties should first attempt to resolve disagreements privately or through direct communication.

The goal is understanding, not winning.

---

### Stage 2 — Community Mediation

If direct communication fails, maintainers or other contributors may assist in facilitating discussion.

Relevant evidence, documentation, and technical context should be considered.

---

### Stage 3 — Governance Review

If the matter affects:

- Architecture
- Governance
- Community integrity
- Foundation operations

it may be escalated for governance review.

A governance review may result in:

- Clarification
- Recommendation
- Binding decision
- Documentation update
- Working group formation

---

## Appeals

Contributors may respectfully challenge decisions when:

- New evidence exists.
- New context becomes available.
- Significant unintended consequences emerge.

Appeals should focus on evidence and reasoning rather than personal criticism.

---

## Institutional Learning

Every major disagreement presents an opportunity to improve:

- Documentation.
- Governance mechanisms.
- Processes.
- Shared understanding.

The objective is not merely to settle disputes.

The objective is institutional learning.

---

## Final Principle

Not every disagreement can be eliminated.

The purpose of governance is not to remove differences.

The purpose of governance is to allow meaningful cooperation despite differences.
