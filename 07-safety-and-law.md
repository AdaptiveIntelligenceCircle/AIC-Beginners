# 07 — Safety and law (orientation)

This file is **orientation**, not legal advice.  
Laws differ by jurisdiction. When in doubt, obtain qualified local advice and prefer restriction over expansion.

---

## Safety posture (design intent)

- **Fail-closed**: under uncertainty, restrict capability and authority.
- **Human gates**: critical transitions should not be silently automated away.
- **Recovery paths**: loss of keys, process failure, or policy triggers should have documented recovery that does not invent new power.
- **No silent bypass**: ethical-kernel and governance constraints are meant to be hard to disable without evidence and process.
- **Under-claim**: public statements should not create false confidence in safety or readiness.

Experimental TestNet and documentation work must not be presented as certified safe for high-stakes use.

---

## Legal orientation (high level)

1. **Entity ≠ immunity**  
   Any organization or individual associated with AIC materials remains subject to applicable law. Documentation of principles does not create legal privilege.

2. **No claim to replace regulation**  
   AIC designs do not supersede national AI regulation, data protection, consumer protection, or criminal law.

3. **Public Digital Infrastructure (PDI) lens**  
   Some materials discuss coordination and meaning infrastructure in a public-interest frame. That is a policy orientation, not a grant of authority or a claim of government endorsement.

4. **Jurisdiction examples (non-exhaustive, non-advice)**  
   - Contributors and users may be subject to the EU AI Act and related updates where applicable.  
   - Vietnam-oriented notes (e.g. AI-related law / decree references in legal policy drafts) are for local awareness, not a compliance certificate.  
   - Other jurisdictions have their own AI, crypto, and intermediary rules. Do not assume one template fits all.

5. **No encouragement of evasion**  
   Do not use AIC materials to plan circumvention of law, sanctions, or safety regulation.

---

## What contributors should do

- Prefer written criteria and checklists over verbal assurances.
- Flag documentation that sounds like a safety or legal guarantee without evidence.
- Keep personal data and sensitive operational details out of public issues unless there is a clear, lawful reason and process.
- For security-sensitive findings, follow the security reporting path of the relevant repository (see SECURITY files where present).

---

## What this file does not do

- It does not certify compliance with any law.
- It does not authorize production deployment.
- It does not bind any court or regulator.
- It does not replace the Covenant criteria or go-live checklists.
